Israel President Taps Gadi Eisenkot to Form Government as Netanyahu Faces Removal

President Isaac Herzog has granted former military chief Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form Israel's next government. The decision threatens Benjamin Netanyahu's long tenure but exposes deep ideological fractures within the opposition alliance.

BS
Blindspot DeskNovember 7, 2026 · 2 min read · Primary source: CNA (Asia)

Key Takeaways

  • President Isaac Herzog tasked former military chief Gadi Eisenkot with forming Israel's next government on November 7.
  • Eisenkot received recommendations from 67 lawmakers, compared to 46 for incumbent Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.
  • The national election took place on October 27 following three years of war and political polarization.
  • Netanyahu faces ongoing corruption charges and an International Criminal Court arrest warrant over the Gaza conflict.

What Happened

President Isaac Herzog announced on November 7 that he would task Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party and former military chief, with forming Israel's next government. The decision followed formal consultations across parliamentary factions after the October 27 national election, which left the country without a clear single-party majority.

According to statements from the president's office and official parliamentary counts, Eisenkot secured recommendations from 67 lawmakers, compared to 46 for incumbent Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The formal appointment ceremony was scheduled at the president's residence in Jerusalem to initiate the legal period for coalition building.

The Context

The political shift follows three years of continuous war and deep polarization under Netanyahu's hard-right administration. Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving prime minister, faces ongoing domestic trial for corruption charges as well as an arrest warrant issued by the International Criminal Court regarding the Gaza conflict, the jurisdiction of which his administration rejects.

In response to the recommendation tally, Netanyahu called for a national unity government limited to self-described Zionist parties, arguing that any alliance dependent on Arab lists would weaken state stability. However, opposition leaders rejected Netanyahu's overtures, reiterating that his removal from office remains a mandatory precondition for governance.

The Blind Spot

While international headlines focus primarily on the potential end of Netanyahu's political era, the underlying arithmetic of Eisenkot's prospective coalition reveals severe structural instability. To assemble a stable majority, Eisenkot's secular liberal Yashar party must reconcile conflicting demands from ultra-Orthodox religious parties and Arab minority list representatives.

This implicit reliance on ideologically divergent blocs creates immediate vulnerabilities. Ultra-Orthodox demands regarding military draft exemptions and state subsidies directly contradict the secular reform platform of Eisenkot's core supporters. Navigating these contradictory domestic pressures risks administrative gridlock and leaves the proposed executive susceptible to collapse over routine legislative votes.

Winners and Losers

Eisenkot and the opposition bloc gain strategic initiative, acquiring executive authority to negotiate cabinet assignments and set the legislative agenda. Israel's Arab minority lists also secure key leverage after elevated voter turnouts translated into crucial recommendatory influence during presidential consultations.

Conversely, Netanyahu and his hard-right coalition partners face political isolation. Stripped of executive office, Netanyahu loses key political mechanisms to counter ongoing judicial proceedings, heightening his exposure to both domestic legal challenges and international legal scrutiny.

What to Watch

Over the coming weeks, attention will center on whether Eisenkot can convert initial party recommendations into a binding coalition agreement before statutory deadlines expire. Key indicators include whether ultra-Orthodox leaders sign formal terms alongside liberal reformers.

Additionally, analysts must monitor whether Netanyahu can exploit divisions within the opposition to break Eisenkot's majority, potentially forcing repeat elections or compelling elements of the center-right into a broad unity coalition.
